Before creating products in Partner Center, decide which **SKUs** (stock-keeping units) you will ship. Each SKU is a distinct product you sell, priced and configured independently. Getting this right up front avoids painful restructuring later — you cannot easily merge or split SKUs once they've had customer entitlements attached.

## Common SKU shapes

| SKU | Composition | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard Edition** | Base game | The default retail SKU. |
| **Deluxe / Gold Edition** | Base game \+ curated DLC pack (skins, weapons, small maps) | Higher price tier, positioned as "the enthusiast option." |
| **Ultimate / Premium Edition** | Base game \+ Season Pass or expansion pass | Highest price tier, bundles everything shipping in year one. |
| **Cross-generation Edition** | Same base game, all supported generations included via Smart Delivery | Common for cross-gen launches. |
| **Deluxe Upgrade** | Add-on that upgrades a Standard Edition owner to Deluxe entitlements | Post-launch upgrade path. |
| **Season Pass** | Add-on granting access to a defined set of future DLC | Predictable DLC revenue. |
| **Digital Deluxe Bundle** | Bundle product containing the base game \+ individual DLC add-ons | Alternative to a monolithic Deluxe Edition. |

## Key decisions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Editions vs bundles vs upgrades">
    An **edition** is a distinct product with its own package. A **bundle** groups already-existing products at a discount (see [Add-ons](/publishing/add-ons)). An **upgrade add-on** grants an entitlement to an existing owner. Each has different customer-facing implications and different Partner Center configuration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Product relationships">
    Decide which SKUs share a product group so a customer who owns one edition doesn't accidentally re-buy another. See [Product relationships](/publishing/product-relationships).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Physical vs digital SKUs">
    A disc release is typically a separate SKU from the digital Standard Edition, even if both contain the same package. See [Discs](/publishing/discs).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Regional variants">
    Most SKUs ship in every market. Some regions (mainland China, Korea) may need dedicated regional SKUs due to licensing or content regulation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trial / demo SKUs">
    If you plan to offer a demo, that is either a Trial on the base SKU or a separate demo product. See [Trials](/publishing/how-to-trials).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## SKU planning checklist

- List every SKU you intend to ship at launch **and** in year one.
- Assign a working name to each SKU.
- Assign a target price tier per region for each SKU.
- Map each SKU to its constituent packages, add-ons, and product-group relationships.
- Confirm the SKU list with your Microsoft representative before creating products in Partner Center.
