## Intent

When a title connects to a network Microsoft does not operate (a third-party matchmaking service, a publisher's own account system, a companion app on a phone, a cross-play network with other platforms), the customer needs to know it and Xbox's privacy privileges must still be honored. XR-007 defines the disclosure, opt-in, and cross-network conduct requirements.

## Applies to

- Titles with **cross-platform multiplayer** (Xbox ↔ PC ↔ PlayStation — any non-Xbox platform).
- Titles with a **publisher-owned account system** used in-game ("log in with PublisherID").
- Titles with a **companion app** on iOS, Android, or web that exchanges data with the game session.
- Titles that use **third-party services** (analytics, ad networks, voice chat services) that leave the Xbox trust boundary.

See also the specific examples: [XR-007 MMO example](/publishing/xr/xr-007-mmo), [XR-007 Multiplayer example](/publishing/xr/xr-007-multiplayer), and [XR-007 (PC)](/publishing/xr/xr-007-pc).

## Requirement

Your title **MUST**:

- **Disclose the cross-network connection** to the customer at the point they enable it (in-game text, EULA acceptance, or first-run notice).
- Honor the customer's **cross-network play privilege** (`XPrivilegeCommunicationsCrossNetworkVoice`, `XPrivilegeCrossNetworkMultiplayer`). If the privilege is denied, do not enable cross-network features.
- Honor **child account restrictions** — accounts flagged as child by Xbox family settings must not enter cross-network play or communications unless the family manager has enabled it.
- Handle the customer's **communications privilege** consistently on Xbox and on the cross-network partner. A customer who has muted communications on Xbox must not receive voice chat from PS5 players.
- Route **any Xbox-network features** (achievements, presence, invites) through Xbox network services rather than a parallel publisher pipeline.
- Present **companion app interactions** with clear disclosure. Companion app data flows must not leak PII (gamertag, XUID) beyond declared scope.
- Route **third-party analytics** through your privacy policy declaration. Third-party ad networks require explicit opt-in.

Your title **MUST NOT**:

- Enable cross-network play by default for accounts that lack the privilege.
- Silently upload gamertag, XUID, or Xbox friend graph to a third-party service without disclosure.
- Bypass Xbox mute / block for cross-network communications.
- Expose child accounts to unmoderated cross-network chat.
- Present cross-network matchmaking as "Xbox only" when it isn't.

## Tested scenarios

- Verify cross-network toggle respects the account's cross-network privilege.
- Verify muting a player on Xbox extends to that player on the cross-network side (best-effort via the publisher network, but must be honored where technically possible).
- Verify a child account with restrictive family settings cannot enter cross-network chat.
- Verify the disclosure UI appears the first time the customer enables cross-network play.
- Verify Xbox-network features (achievements, presence, invites) still work when cross-network is on.
- For companion apps: verify data flows described in the app's privacy policy are what actually flows.

## Best practices

- **Design the cross-network privilege check at the gate**, not deep in matchmaking. A customer without the privilege should never see "Searching for match..." — they should see the disclosure and, if applicable, the opt-in.
- **Coordinate mute / block across networks** via your matchmaking service. Where cross-network mute isn't technically feasible, disclose that clearly.
- **Isolate child accounts** at the friend-graph level — they shouldn't even receive cross-network invite offers.
- **Document the third-party services** used in your privacy policy (see [Properties: Basic info](/publishing/properties-basic-info)).
