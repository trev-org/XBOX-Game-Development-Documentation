## What session-based multiplayer makes hard

Session-based multiplayer titles (shooters, fighting games, racing games with lobbies, party games) with cross-platform play typically have:

- **Matchmaking** that pools players across Xbox, PC, PlayStation, mobile.
- **Invite flows** where an Xbox player can invite a non-Xbox friend into a session.
- **Voice chat** during a match with players on other platforms.
- **Post-match reporting** to a publisher backend.

Each of these interacts with Xbox network privileges and Xbox privacy features. This page is the applied guidance.

## Cross-platform matchmaking

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the cross-network privilege before matchmaking">
    Before showing the customer a "Find match" button that will place them in a cross-platform lobby, verify their `XPrivilegeCrossNetworkMultiplayer` privilege. Accounts without the privilege must be routed to Xbox-only matchmaking or shown a clear message.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Route the child-account case explicitly">
    Child accounts (Xbox family-managed) must not be silently placed in cross-platform lobbies. Present family-safe options.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provide an in-game 'Cross-play' toggle">
    Even for accounts with the privilege, expose a per-title cross-play toggle so customers can choose Xbox-only play. Persist their choice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Handle mixed sessions gracefully">
    If a session is mid-match and a cross-network player disconnects, the session must not crash or corrupt state.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Invite flows

- **Xbox → non-Xbox invites** — the Xbox player invokes an Xbox invite UI. Your session code translates that to a cross-network invite via your matchmaking backend. See [XR-124 Game Invitations](/publishing/fma/xr-124).
- **Non-Xbox → Xbox invites** — the invite arrives via your backend and surfaces in the game's own invite UI. Also mirror it to the Xbox invite surface where possible.
- **Party chat integration** — Xbox Party Chat is the customer's chosen voice channel; do not force in-game voice on top of party chat without an explicit opt-in.

## Voice chat with non-Xbox players

This is the most common XR-007 friction point.

- Check `XPrivilegeCommunicationsCrossNetworkVoice` before enabling cross-network voice.
- Enforce the **Xbox mute list** on incoming voice from cross-network players. If your matchmaking backend can transmit the muted XUID list to the other platform's client, do that; if not, mute on the Xbox side so the Xbox customer never hears the muted party.
- Enforce **child account** voice restrictions — no voice chat with strangers for accounts flagged as child.
- Provide **in-session mute controls** consistent across platforms.

## Post-match reporting to publisher backend

- **What you can send**: match results, stats, custom telemetry per your privacy policy.
- **What you cannot send silently**: PII, gamertag mappings to third-party IDs beyond what's disclosed, Xbox friend graph, private communications content.
- If you use a publisher account, keep Xbox account data scoped tightly to the linked account.
