UserStoreIds are signed with a rotating publisher-scoped key. When Microsoft rotates the signing key, previously issued UserStoreIds may become invalid. Your service must recover automatically.

## Symptoms of rotation

- A previously valid UserStoreId starts returning `401 InvalidUserStoreId` (or `AuthenticationFailed`) from `/publisherQuery`, `/consume`, `/orders/query`, or `/recurrences/query`.
- The client and service happily agreed on the UserStoreId minutes earlier; only now is it rejected.

## Recovery pattern

1. Detect the `401` in your service.
2. Return a specific error to the client asking for a fresh UserStoreId (do **not** return the raw Store error).
3. The client re-invokes `XStoreGetUserCollectionsIdAsync` (or `XStoreGetUserPurchaseIdAsync`) to fetch a new UserStoreId.
4. The client resends the operation with the new UserStoreId.
5. Service retries the Store call.

Because the client-side call is fast (no user interaction), this loop is transparent to the player.

## Prevent unnecessary rotation calls

- Do not fetch a UserStoreId until you actually need it. Keys are one-shot in intent.
- Never cache UserStoreIds across app sessions or across user sign-out.
- When your service ingests a UserStoreId, use it and discard it — don't hold it in a queue for later.

## Instrumentation

Log every `401 InvalidUserStoreId` with the timestamp and the AAD app / product context. A spike in these errors typically signals a Microsoft-side rotation event; a slow trickle typically signals your service is caching UserStoreIds too long.
