Use [XDSP](/gdk/audio/xdsp) to shrink an impulse response (IR) in length while preserving its perceptual character. Reducing an IR that would otherwise be thousands of taps long dramatically cuts runtime convolution cost.

## When to use it

- Convolution reverbs where full-length IRs are prohibitively expensive.
- Guitar-cab or environmental IRs captured at high resolution.
- Middleware pipelines that need to ship compact IR variants for lower-power targets.

## Pipeline

<Steps>
  <Step title="Configure the reduction target">
    Decide the target length (in samples or taps) and quality parameters. Trade-off: shorter IR = less DSP cost but a possible loss of tail detail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the IR-reduction command">
    Queue the reduction command with source, destination, target length, and quality settings. Optionally set `SignalOnCompletion`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Best practices

- Reduce IRs **offline** where possible and ship the reduced IR with the title, not the raw capture.
- Reuse the XDSP client across multiple reductions in a batch — client creation is expensive.
- Validate the reduced IR audibly against reference material — automated metrics don't always align with perceived quality.
- 16-byte alignment on source and destination buffers is required.
