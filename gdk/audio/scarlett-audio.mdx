Xbox Series X|S audio design principles cover four hardware-accelerated components. This page introduces the audio decoder, high-quality sample rate converter (HSRC), and future acoustics work.

## Decoding audio

Audio decoding on Series X|S improves pitch, quality, and sampling for your title's audio. For general Xbox audio guidance, see the [Audio overview](/gdk/audio/overview).

## Opus codec

The Xbox One family used XMA for hardware-offload decoding. Xbox Series X|S uses **Opus** hardware-offload decoding via the Xbox Audio Processing Unit (XAPU) API. See [XAPU overview](/gdk/audio/xapu).

Opus is a royalty-free audio compression codec designed to efficiently code speech and general audio into a single low-latency format. Internal evaluations show Opus surpasses XMA in quality and file-size compression ratio. Opus combines Skype's SILK codec and [Xilph.Org's CELT codec](https://wiki.xiph.org/CELT_LCA).

<Note>
  Opus is computationally expensive on CPU when running many simultaneous streams. Series X|S offloads this to a dedicated co-processor — you get premium codec quality without spending CPU cycles.
</Note>

Compared to Xbox One, Series X|S eliminates one memory hop between the audio decoder output and the HSRC — delivering audio to users with minimal latency.

### Opus vs XMA noise reduction

At 64-bit, XMA quality and noise degrade noticeably. Opus at 64-bit matches XMA at 128-bit.

<Tip>
  To match Opus 64-bit noise reduction quality with XMA, set XMA to 128-bit.
</Tip>

## High-Quality Sample Rate Convertor (HSRC)

The HSRC works with Opus hardware decoding to change pitch. Output quality is improved compared to SHAPE.

<Note>
  SHAPE is still supported on Series X|S for backward-compatibility titles.
</Note>

## Acoustics

Future releases will add features to further enhance sound encoding and decoding quality on Series X|S consoles.
