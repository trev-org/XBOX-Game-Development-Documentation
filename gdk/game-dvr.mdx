Game DVR records gameplay clips and screenshots on XBOX and PC. Use [XAppCapture](/reference/xappcapture) to trigger captures and tag metadata.

## Capture types

- **Manual** — player triggers via the XBOX shell or share button.
- **Title-initiated** — your title programmatically captures a clip or screenshot at a key moment.
- **Broadcasting** — live streaming to Twitch / supported services.

## Metadata tagging

Tag captures with `XAppCapture` metadata so highlights are easier to find in the player's gallery (boss kills, achievements, milestones).

## Broadcasting

Broadcasting captures and streams gameplay live to supported services (e.g., Twitch) through the XBOX shell:

- Players start a broadcast from the **share** button or guide
- Your title cannot start or stop a broadcast directly; the shell owns the user-facing flow
- Use `XGameUserPresenceWriter` and the broadcast state APIs to detect when a broadcast is in progress and adjust telemetry, voice chat exclusion, and HUD privacy as needed
- Privacy: chat-only HUD elements (player names, sensitive UI) should respect the broadcast state and hide identifying content where appropriate

## Excluding voice chat from captures

User voice chat must never be captured into clips or broadcasts. Set the `AudioCategory_GameChat` category on any XAudio2 source voice or WASAPI render stream that carries microphone audio so the capture pipeline excludes it automatically. See [Audio](/gdk/audio) for the integration pattern.

## Capture restrictions

Capture is gated by several user and content controls. Always check before assuming a capture will land:

- **User privilege** — `XPRIVILEGE_GAME_DVR`. Children, restricted accounts, and guests may not capture
- **Title metadata** — your `MicrosoftGame.config` can disable capture per-segment for cutscenes or pre-rendered content that should not be re-shared
- **Storage** — the player may be out of space on the share device; surface a friendly notification rather than silently failing
- **Concurrency** — only one broadcast or one explicit clip can be in flight at a time; queue subsequent requests
