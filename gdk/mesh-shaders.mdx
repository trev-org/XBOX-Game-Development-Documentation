Mesh shaders replace the legacy vertex / geometry / tessellation pipeline with two new stages: **amplification** and **mesh**.

## Why use them

- Cull at meshlet granularity, before the rasterizer.
- Procedurally generate geometry on the GPU without round-trip CPU buffers.
- Bypass input-assembler limits for huge instanced scenes.

## Best practices on XBOX Series X|S

- Pack vertices into ~64-vertex / ~126-primitive meshlets.
- Use the amplification shader for instance culling, LOD selection, and meshlet visibility tests.
- Combine with [VRS](/gdk/vrs) and [sampler feedback](/gdk/sampler-feedback) for further savings.

## API design

The mesh-shader pipeline has two HLSL entry points and a fixed export contract:

- **Amplification shader (AS)** — runs first; reads instance data, performs coarse culling and LOD selection, and **dispatches** mesh shader threadgroups via `DispatchMesh`
- **Mesh shader (MS)** — emits up to 256 vertices and 256 primitives per threadgroup using `SetMeshOutputCounts`, `vertices[]`, `indices[]`, and `primitives[]`
- **Payload** — the AS hands a packed payload struct to each MS dispatch; keep it small (\< 16 KB)

The MS replaces the entire VS/HS/DS/GS chain. Pixel shader binding and PSO state are unchanged.

## Pipeline usage

Typical render pass:

1. CPU builds a list of meshlets per draw call (offline, in your authoring pipeline)
2. Bind PSO with AS \+ MS stages; bind a structured buffer of meshlets and instance data
3. `ExecuteIndirect` or `DispatchMesh` to kick the AS
4. AS culls instances and dispatches MS threadgroups
5. MS emits triangles directly to the rasterizer

No vertex buffers, no index buffer, no input assembler. Indirect arguments are minimal.

## Meshlet rendering

A **meshlet** is a small cluster of triangles that fits in one MS threadgroup. Typical sizes:

- 64 vertices, 126 primitives per meshlet (the platform sweet spot on Series X|S)
- Author offline with a meshlet generator (open-source `meshoptimizer` works well)
- Store meshlets contiguously with shared vertex data and per-meshlet bounding info (sphere or cone) used for culling

## Meshlet culling

The amplification shader is where the win comes from:

- **Frustum culling** at meshlet granularity — reject thousands of meshlets per frame
- **Backface cone culling** — reject meshlets whose normal cone faces away from the camera
- **Occlusion culling** — feed last frame's Hi-Z or sampler feedback to skip occluded meshlets
- **LOD selection** — pick a meshlet LOD per cluster based on projected size, swap meshlets in/out without CPU work

## Mesh shaders on Xbox Series X|S

Specifics for the platform:

- **Wave size** — Series X|S optimally runs MS at the platform's native wave width; keep threadgroup sizes a multiple of the wave to avoid divergence
- **Bandwidth-first design** — avoid wide reads inside the MS; do them in the AS and pass results in the payload
- **Async with compute** — interleave mesh-shader passes with compute work to fill the GPU
- **Tooling** — PIX **Mesh Viewer** introspects emitted vertices and primitives per meshlet; use it to debug culling and emission rates
