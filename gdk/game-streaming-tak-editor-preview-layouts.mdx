One of the strongest features of the TAK Editor is previewing a layout against real gameplay video. Do this before every packing pass — it catches occlusion, unreachable controls, and cluttered composition that are invisible on an empty canvas.

## Set up the preview

<Steps>
  <Step title="Record a gameplay clip">
    Capture a short clip that covers the gameplay states each layout targets. Use the client resolution your target audience will play at.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Load the clip">
    In the editor, open the preview panel and pick your video file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the layout to preview">
    Choose the layout whose overlay you want to inspect on top of the video.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scrub through gameplay">
    Step through the video and check that every control remains reachable, visible, and non-conflicting with the underlying HUD.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to check

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Reach" icon="hand">
    Every high-frequency control should sit under a thumb that is already there. Rarely-used controls can sit slightly further out.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Occlusion of HUD" icon="eye-slash">
    Confirm the layout does not hide critical HUD elements — health bars, minimap, timer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Visibility over the scene" icon="circle-half-stroke">
    Check contrast against dark and light frames. Turn on the [high contrast preview](/gdk/game-streaming-tak-high-contrast-mode) to verify accessibility.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Localization overflow" icon="language">
    Switch the preview language to your longest-string language and confirm labels still fit.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Iterating

Make changes to the layout without closing the preview — the overlay updates as you edit. Use short clips per gameplay state so you can compare a layout against exactly the state it targets.
