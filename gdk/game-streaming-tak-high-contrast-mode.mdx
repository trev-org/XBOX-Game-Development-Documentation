When a streamed client requests high-contrast mode, the touch adaptation runtime prefers the high-contrast variant of every asset. Ship this variant whenever you ship [custom assets](/gdk/game-streaming-tak-custom-assets), and rely on the default high-contrast art otherwise.

## What high contrast changes

- Higher luminance separation between controls and background.
- Simplified silhouettes with fewer intermediate values.
- Removed decorative detail that reduces legibility at high compression.
- Strong outlines on interactive elements so their bounds are unambiguous.

## Providing high-contrast assets

<Steps>
  <Step title="Author high-contrast art">
    Produce a paired PNG for every custom asset with high luminance contrast, strong outlines, and reduced intermediate values.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store under an hc/ subfolder">
    Place high-contrast art alongside the default under `assets/hc/`. Match the filename of the default asset so the runtime picks the paired variant.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify in the editor">
    Preview the layout with high contrast enabled in the [TAK Editor](/gdk/game-streaming-tak-editor).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify on device">
    Enable the client OS's high-contrast setting on a real device and confirm the runtime swaps to the paired variant during a streaming session.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What good looks like

- Each control still reads as itself at a glance — do not simply invert the default art.
- Interactive vs. decorative elements are visually distinct.
- The layout remains usable without color — verify by desaturating a screenshot.

<Warning>
  Do not skip high-contrast art when you ship custom assets. The runtime cannot generate a high-contrast variant from a stylized custom asset, and the fallback experience is worse than either the custom art or the default high-contrast art in isolation.
</Warning>
