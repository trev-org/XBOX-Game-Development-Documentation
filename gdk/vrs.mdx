VRS lets you reduce pixel shader work in regions of the frame where full-rate shading isn't needed.

## VRS tiers on XBOX

- **Tier 1** — per-draw shading rate.
- **Tier 2** — per-primitive and screen-space shading-rate images.

## Common uses

- **Foveated rendering** — full rate in the center, coarser at the edges.
- **Motion-based VRS** — coarser rate where motion vectors are large.
- **Content-adaptive VRS** — coarser rate in low-contrast areas.

## Why VRS

Pixel shader cost dominates frame time in most modern engines. VRS lets you spend that cost where the eye notices:

- Foveal regions, foreground characters, weapon hands — full rate
- Periphery, blurred backgrounds, motion-blurred regions — coarser rate
- Typical savings: 10–30% of pixel-shader cost with no perceptible quality loss

## Feature design

VRS on Series X|S supports three combiners:

1. **Per-draw rate** — set with `RSSetShadingRate`; applies to the whole draw
2. **Per-primitive rate** — emitted by the vertex / mesh shader as `SV_ShadingRate`
3. **Screen-space rate image** — a small texture mapped to the framebuffer, selects rate per tile

The three sources combine through a configurable combiner (`Override`, `Min`, `Max`, `Sum`) — typical pipelines use min/passthrough at draw, then max at the image stage.

## API usage

- Bind the shading-rate image via `RSSetShadingRateImage`
- Author the rate image at 1/16 of the framebuffer resolution (16×16 pixel tiles)
- Refresh the image per frame from motion vectors \+ screen-space contrast
- Use the per-draw rate to opt entire post-process passes into half-rate
