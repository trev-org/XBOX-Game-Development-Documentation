Drive RGB lighting on compatible peripherals from your title via the GDK [Lighting API](/reference/lighting). Dynamic lighting on the GDK is built on `Windows.Devices.Lights.LampArray` and works across controllers, keyboards, mice, and headsets that expose addressable LEDs.

## What you can do

  <Card title="Enumerate peripherals" icon="microchip">
    Discover every compatible `LampArray` device and its per-lamp positional metadata.

  <Card title="Push per-LED colors" icon="palette">

  <Card title="Queue effect playlists" icon="wave-square">

## The model

- **LampArray** — a per-device collection of addressable LEDs with positional metadata.
- **Lamp** — a single addressable light. Each lamp exposes its purpose (indicator, accent, key) and 3D position on the device.
- **Effect playlist** — queue color changes and have the system interpolate between them.
- **Ownership** — your title takes foreground ownership of a `LampArray` while focused; the OS restores ambient state when you lose focus.

<Note>
  The platform enforces global user preference. If the player has disabled dynamic lighting in Settings, your writes are dropped. Always check `IsAvailable` before authoring elaborate effects.
</Note>

## Working with colors

- Author lighting in **linear sRGB**, then let the runtime apply the device's per-LED gamma curve.
- Provide a per-lamp purpose mask (indicator vs accent) — players can opt out of any one class.
- Respect the per-lamp **brightness ceiling** the device reports; many keyboards cap brightness in firmware.
- Use the lamp position metadata to drive directional effects (impact flashes from the hit direction).

## Effects

Typical effects ship as composable presets:

| Effect | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Solid** | Team color, season palette, ambient idle |
| **Pulse** | Low-health warning, ability cooldown, comms incoming |
| **Wave** | Damage absorbed, shield recharge |
| **Ripple** | Hit feedback radiating from the impact point |
| **Color cycle** | Menu / loading idle |

Author effects as data, not code — store effect templates in your title data so designers can tune timing and palette without engine rebuilds.

## Best practices

<Steps>
  <Step title="Have an idle pattern">
    Keep an ambient pattern running so devices do not go dark when nothing is happening.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Throttle updates">
    60–90 Hz is plenty. Anything higher is wasted bandwidth and can be dropped by the driver.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fall back gracefully">
    Older OS versions, non-RGB devices, and opted-out users all end up in the same silent-drop path. Do not gate gameplay on lighting output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Author effects as data">
    Effect timing and palette should live in title data. Designers should not need an engine rebuild to tune a pulse.
  </Step>
</Steps>
