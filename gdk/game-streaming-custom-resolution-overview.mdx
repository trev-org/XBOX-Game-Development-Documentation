The streaming pipeline can run your title at a non-native resolution to fit different client targets. Custom resolution support is required for the smallest-bandwidth streaming tiers and is recommended for handheld clients.

## Why custom resolution exists

- Not all streaming clients can display 1080p; some are targeted at 720p to reduce bandwidth.
- Different aspect ratios (portrait phones, ultrawide displays) benefit from a render target that already matches the client shape.
- Encoding at the client's native resolution avoids a rescale on the client side, which typically improves perceived quality.

## What your title must do

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the negotiated resolution">
    Query [`XGameStreamingGetClientDimensions`](/gdk/game-streaming-am-i-streaming) at session start. Do not assume 1080p.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-allocate render targets on change">
    A client can reconnect at a different resolution. Re-allocate render targets and post-process buffers when the reported dimensions change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scale UI">
    Adjust HUD, menu, and text scale so they remain legible at each supported client dimension.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Avoid hard-coded resolutions">
    Remove hard-coded resolutions from shaders, post-process kernels, and texture mip selection. Derive them from the current render target.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What is supported

| Client family | Common negotiated resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Web / Windows CTA on a PC | 1080p |
| Smart TVs | 1080p |
| Higher-tier phones and tablets | 1080p |
| Lower-tier or low-bandwidth phones | 720p |
| Portrait touch clients | Adjusted aspect |

Specific values are negotiated per client and per session; treat them as data, not constants.
