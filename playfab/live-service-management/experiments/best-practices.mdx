Experiments are the gold standard for measuring the effect of game-experience changes. Backing decisions with data lets you craft more effective design, gameplay, and marketing — and detect when a change's benefit fades over time.

## Why experiment

- Move from _we think_ to _we know_.
- Protect the wider player base from bad experiences by exposing changes to a limited audience first.
- Enable or disable features safely in a live game.
- Focus resources on what demonstrably works.

## Common goals

- Increase engagement (session length, sessions per DAU).
- Improve retention (Day N return).
- Grow monetization (payer conversion, ARPU).
- Boost acquisition (funnel completion).

## Best practices

- **Define your metric first.** Pick your primary success metric before designing variants.
- **Ensure practical significance, not just statistical significance.** A 0.1% lift with a huge sample may pass a p-value test yet not be worth shipping.
- **Run for full weekly cycles.** Weekday vs. weekend behavior differs.
- **Guard against Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM).** If actual traffic split deviates significantly from configured split, stop and investigate before trusting results.
- **Use exclusion groups for overlapping funnels.**
- **Don't peek and stop early.** Predefine duration.
- **Document hypotheses.** Every experiment should have a hypothesis you can accept or reject.

## Resolving SRM

If the scorecard flags SRM, check: variant delivery bugs, differential opt-outs, filter differences between arms, or bot traffic hitting one variant more than another.
