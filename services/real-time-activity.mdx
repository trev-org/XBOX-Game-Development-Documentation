The **Real-Time Activity (RTA)** service is XBOX's push channel — a persistent WebSocket over which your title receives notifications for presence, multiplayer session state, event-based stat changes, and invites, all without polling.

When you use XSAPI's Social Manager, Presence, or Multiplayer subscriptions, RTA is what powers them under the hood.

## How RTA works

```text
Your title ──► XSAPI ──► WebSocket ──► RTA endpoint ──► XBOX services
     ▲          │
     └── event ─┘  (topic message)
```

- Your title opens a **single RTA channel per signed-in user**.
- You subscribe to **topics** — presence, session, stat, MPA — on that channel.
- The service pushes a message whenever the underlying data changes.
- XSAPI dispatches the message to your registered handler on the callback queue you specified.

XSAPI manages the socket: reconnect with exponential backoff, resubscribe after reconnect, heartbeats, and back-pressure. You do not implement the WebSocket protocol yourself.

## What you can subscribe to

- **Presence** — device presence (`XblPresenceSubscribeToDevicePresenceChange`), title presence (`XblPresenceSubscribeToTitlePresenceChange`).
- **Multiplayer sessions** — session state changes (`XblMultiplayerSetSubscriptionsEnabled` \+ `XblMultiplayerAddSessionChangedHandler`).
- **Event-based stats** — `XblStatisticSubscribeToStatisticChange` fires when the derived stat updates.
- **Social graph changes** — Social Manager surfaces relationship / favorite / block changes as event group updates.
- **Invites** — invite notifications are delivered via RTA and surfaced through `XGameInvite` protocol activation.

## Register for stat notifications

A common RTA use case is reacting to your **own** stat updates for instant HUD refresh:

```cpp
XblRealTimeActivitySubscriptionHandle statSub = nullptr;
HRESULT hr = XblStatisticSubscribeToStatisticChange(
    xblContext,
    xuid,
    scid,
    "Kills",
    &statSub);

XblStatisticAddStatisticChangedHandler(
    xblContext,
    [](XblStatisticChangeEventArgs args, void* ctx) {
        // Fires on the callback task queue when the stat changes
    },
    /* context */ nullptr);
```

Unsubscribe (`XblStatisticUnsubscribeFromStatisticChange`) when the UI is torn down or the user signs out.

## Best practices

- **One channel per user** — XSAPI already enforces this; do not open parallel channels.
- **Coalesce handlers** — a rapid burst of events (e.g. many friends signing in at once) should not fan out to N UI redraws. Batch on your side and refresh the view once per frame or per interval.
- **Do not do heavy work on the callback thread** — dispatch to a background `XTaskQueue` if you need to update engine state.
- **Handle disconnect / reconnect** — XSAPI reconnects for you, but your title should treat a disconnect as "data may be stale" and refresh key views on reconnect.
- **Unsubscribe cleanly** — leaked subscriptions keep firing until the channel closes; explicitly release them on scope end.
- **Test flaky networks** — use Dev Home's network simulator or unplug the NIC to confirm your UI handles a temporary RTA outage.

## Programming the RTA service

Most titles never call the RTA service directly — they use the XSAPI subscription helpers above. For advanced scenarios (custom topics from your own service, S2S RTA usage) the raw RTA API is available:

- Open a channel with `XblRealTimeActivityActivateAsync`.
- Subscribe / unsubscribe with `XblRealTimeActivityAddConnectionStateChangeHandler` and topic-specific `Subscribe*` calls.
- Handle connection-state changes explicitly (`Connecting`, `Connected`, `Disconnected`).
