Events increasingly build their own websites and apps for schedules, maps, and networking. These often get overlooked from an accessibility perspective. Bake accessibility in during concept phase and, when using a third party, confirm their conformance early — ask for a [VPAT](https://www.section508.gov/sell/vpat/).

Accessible digital experiences take time to build and validate. Don't wait until it's too late.

## Scoping questions

This guideline applies if you answer **yes** to either:

- Does your event have a website?
- Does your event use a companion app?

## Implementation guidelines

### Websites

**Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)**

- Websites should meet [WCAG 2.1 AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21).

**Alternative methods**

- Provide an alternative way to access the site's materials — printed handouts, brochures, or physical maps.
- If the site includes functionality like scheduling meetings or messaging guests, offer alternative channels such as staff-assisted service at an information desk.

### Applications

**Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)**

- Apps should follow [WCAG 2.1 AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21) where applicable.

**Alternative methods**

- Provide alternative access to the app's materials — printed handouts, maps, brochures.
- If the app includes networking or messaging features, offer staff-assisted alternatives.
